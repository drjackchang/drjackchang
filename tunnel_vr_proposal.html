摘要

中文摘要

隧道環境救援任務具有空間侷限、高溫濃煙、能見度低、逃生路徑複雜等特性，對執行任務之搜救人員構成顯著之心理壓力，長期而言可能影響其臨場判斷、行動效率與心理韌性，甚至導致創傷後壓力反應。然而目前對搜救人員（相對於一般疏散民眾）在隧道情境下之心理壓力歷程，仍缺乏可控、可重複之實驗場域進行系統性測量、行為科學分析與事前訓練介入。本研究將以團隊已完成之隧道環境360度影像資料為基礎，透過攝影測量技術重建為可互動之三維虛擬實境（VR）隧道場域，並整合火煙特效模擬，建立一套可調控情境變因之心理壓力誘發與測量平台，並以行為科學觀點分析壓力狀態下之決策與行為改變。本研究將與日本Dr. Miho Seike合作，比較台灣與日本兩地搜救人員於相同VR隧道情境下之心理壓力反應與應變行為，並評估VR暴露訓練對強化心理準備之成效。

Abstract

Tunnel rescue operations present unique psychological challenges due to confined space, dense smoke, poor visibility, and complex evacuation routes, placing significant stress on rescue personnel — as distinct from general evacuees — that can affect real-time judgment, operational efficiency, and psychological resilience. Building on 360° footage of an actual tunnel already captured by our team, this project reconstructs the environment into an interactive VR space to create a controllable platform for inducing and measuring psychological stress, in collaboration with Dr. Miho Seike (Hiroshima University), an internationally recognized researcher on psychological stress in tunnel evacuation.

一、研究動機與重要性

隧道、地下空間等封閉環境的火災與坍塌事故，因逃生路徑單一、濃煙擴散快速、通訊與定位困難，長期被消防與緊急救護體系列為高風險任務類型。第一線人員在此情境下除面對生理負荷外，更承受高強度心理壓力；依壓力因應交易理論（Transactional Model of Stress and Coping）之觀點（Lazarus & Folkman, 1984），此壓力狀態若未經適當之認知評估調適與訓練，可能影響其決策品質、團隊溝通與長期心理健康。

目前台灣與日本在隧道搜救訓練上多仍仰賴實體演練場地，受限於場地取得、安全風險與可重複性，難以在受控條件下系統性測量人員之心理壓力反應。VR技術之發展使可控、可重複、低風險之情境模擬訓練成為可能，國際上已有研究證實VR之多感官情境模擬可有效提升疏散情境之緊迫感知與行為真實性（Shaw et al., 2019），也提供跨國以相同刺激情境進行比較研究的機會。

本計畫團隊已完成隧道實景之360度影像拍攝，並具備將其重建為可互動VR場域之技術基礎。值得特別說明的是，隧道搜救情境對「執行任務之搜救人員」與「受困之一般用路人／民眾」所引發的心理歷程並不相同：既有國際文獻（如Li et al., 2022, 2024a, 2024b之隧道疏散研究）多以「一般用路人」為對象，其核心反應為逃生求生；搜救人員則須在承受相近環境壓力之同時，持續進行任務判斷、器材操作與團隊協作，其心理韌性與抗壓表現直接關係搜救成效與自身安全。本計畫聚焦於「搜救人員」而非一般用路人之心理壓力反應，並以行為科學觀點（詳見理論架構，特別是Easterbrook, 1959之線索利用理論）探討壓力狀態下之注意力、決策與行為改變歷程。

二、文獻回顧

（一）國外相關研究：Dr. Miho Seike 團隊

日本廣島大學（現職，過去與筑波大學有淵源）Dr. Miho Seike 為隧道火災疏散行為與心理壓力量測領域之國際重要學者。其團隊近年運用實尺寸隧道充煙疏散實驗，量測受試者之疏散步行速度與心理／情緒狀態，並以結構方程模式（SEM）分析負向情緒與步行行為之關聯性（Li et al., 2024a——《Negative emotion degree in smoke filled tunnel evacuation》, Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology; Li et al., 2024b——《Structural equation modeling of negative emotion and walking behavior》, Safety Science），近期並發表以機器學習方法解析隧道疏散情境下人員情緒反應之研究（Seike et al., 2024），以及結合步行速度與心理狀態之全尺寸實驗（Li et al., 2026）。

特別值得注意的是，該團隊已發表以中國與日本用路人為對象、比較兩國人員於隧道火災情境下疏散行為與心理壓力反應之跨國研究（Li et al., 2022），顯示其團隊具備執行跨國比較研究之實務經驗與方法論基礎，與本計畫欲進行之台日比較設計高度呼應。此外，Dr. Seike過去亦曾參與台灣高雄過港隧道機車道之火災安全評估研究，並直接以2012年雪山隧道火燒車事件之影像分析作為研究起點（Chung et al., 2022），另有隧道火災熱釋放率相關研究（Seike et al., 2019），顯示其與台灣隧道議題已有直接研究連結。

惟其既有研究對象多為「一般用路人／疏散民眾」，尚未針對「執行搜救任務之消防／救護人員」在隧道情境下之心理壓力反應與事前訓練需求進行系統性研究，此為本計畫可與其研究形成互補、並作為延伸合作切入點之關鍵所在。

（二）國內相關研究

台灣現有消防人員心理壓力相關研究多聚焦一般勤務情境，例如以中文版戴氏創傷量表（DTS）調查到院前心肺功能停止（OHCA）案件與消防人員創傷後壓力症候群（PTSD）危險因子之橫斷式研究（陳豐仁，2019），以及消防人員工作壓力、休閒調適與身心健康關聯之問卷研究（吳偉民、陳餘鋆，2020），方法上多以量表調查為主，較少針對特定高風險情境建立可控之情境模擬並輔以生理指標測量。另一方面，國內對公路隧道之研究則多集中於工程與管理面向，例如國道雪山隧道之災害應變及救援標準作業程序建制（陳發林，交通部高速公路局委託研究，年份待查證），研究對象同樣以「用路人／乘客」疏散安全為主，較少觸及執行搜救任務人員之心理層面。綜合上述，國內目前尚缺乏結合VR情境模擬、生理心理量測與搜救人員訓練介入之整合性研究，本計畫可望填補此一空缺。

（三）建議邀請之國內顧問：張賢龢教授

美國安柏瑞德航天大學安全與應急機關管理學系助理教授張賢龢，2002–2010年任職台灣消防單位期間時任新北市消防局，為雪山隧道火災應變計畫之負責人，現專長美國事故指揮體系（ICS）與台美災害應變體系比較。其「應變體系管理」層次之專長，與Dr. Seike之「個體疏散心理與行為」研究、以及本計畫之「VR訓練介入」形成三個互補層次，建議邀請其擔任國內顧問。

三、本計畫之研究缺口與創新性

  • 對象創新：聚焦「執行搜救任務之人員」而非一般疏散民眾，與Dr. Seike既有研究（Li et al., 2022, 2024a, 2024b）形成互補而非重疊
  • 方法創新：以VR取代真實充煙隧道實驗，可控制情境變因並大幅降低受試者實際暴露風險，同時參考多感官模擬文獻（Shaw et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2024）保留高沉浸感之心理壓力誘發效果
  • 應用創新：依壓力免疫訓練理論（Meichenbaum, 1985）將VR平台轉化為可重複操作之事前心理準備訓練工具
  • 比較創新：延續Dr. Seike團隊既有跨國比較研究之方法基礎（Li et al., 2022），將比較對象由一般用路人擴展至專業搜救人員

四、理論架構

本計畫整合三層理論，建構貫穿「環境壓力源→行為表現」與「訓練介入」兩條路徑之整體架構。

4.1 總體框架：壓力因應交易理論（Lazarus & Folkman, 1984）

此理論主張心理壓力並非環境刺激直接造成，而是由「初級評估」（此情境對我是否構成威脅）與「次級評估」（我是否具備因應資源）交互中介而成，是壓力研究中最具代表性之經典框架（Lazarus & Folkman, 1984）。

4.2 延伸Dr. Seike既有實證模型

Dr. Seike團隊已以結構方程模式（SEM）驗證「負向情緒→步行速度」之路徑關係（Li et al., 2024b）。本計畫將此路徑向前延伸，加入認知評估階段作為前置變項，並向後將行為結果由單純之疏散步行速度，擴展為專業任務行為（決策正確率、器材操作表現）。此延伸策略之優點在於：計畫並非憑空提出新模型，而是在既有國際發表之實證路徑上，清楚標示本計畫之延伸貢獻。此外，Dr. Seike團隊已發表之效果量將進一步作為本計畫貝氏統計分析（詳見6.6）之資訊性先驗，使既有實證知識能正式轉化為模型估計的一部分，而非僅止於文字引用。

4.3 訓練介入框架：壓力免疫訓練（Meichenbaum, 1985）

此為臨床心理學中用於建立心理韌性之經典應用理論，包含三階段：概念化教育、技能習得與演練、應用與追蹤（Meichenbaum, 1985）。此框架可直接對應本計畫「VR心理準備訓練」介入設計之三個階段。

4.4 補充變項：自我效能理論（Bandura, 1997）

建議將自我效能納入訓練前後測之心理量表，作為除生理與行為指標外之第三種訓練成效證據（Bandura, 1997），並可作為認知評估與因應行為間之中介變項進行後續統計分析。

4.5 延伸測量面向：注意力窄化與視覺搜索行為（Easterbrook, 1959）

Easterbrook之線索利用理論主張，情緒喚起程度升高會使個體可利用之線索範圍縮減，此現象常用於解釋高壓情境下專業人員資訊蒐集與決策品質下降之機制（Easterbrook, 1959）。若採用具備眼動追蹤功能之VR頭盔（詳見六、6.7），可量測受試者凝視點分布範圍、掃視路徑複雜度等指標，作為驗證圖2模型之額外行為科學證據。

4.6 研究假設

  • H1：環境壓力源強度與受試者之威脅認知評估程度呈正相關（Lazarus & Folkman, 1984）
  • H2：威脅認知評估程度與生理喚起指標及主觀負向情緒呈正相關
  • H3：生理與情緒反應程度與任務行為表現呈負相關，此路徑延伸自Dr. Seike團隊既有SEM實證模型（Li et al., 2024b）
  • H4：接受VR心理準備訓練之受試者，其訓練後之心理壓力反應顯著低於訓練前，且自我效能顯著提升（Meichenbaum, 1985; Bandura, 1997）
  • H5：台灣與日本受試者於相同VR情境下之心理壓力反應與任務行為表現模式存在差異，以貝氏後驗可信區間（credible interval）重疊程度判定（呼應Li et al., 2022之跨國比較發現）

五、研究目的

  • 建置可互動、可調控情境變因之隧道VR心理壓力誘發暨測試平台
  • 建立搜救人員心理壓力生理與主觀測量之標準化施測流程
  • 以行為科學觀點分析搜救人員於高壓情境下之注意力、決策與行為改變歷程
  • 比較台灣與日本兩地搜救人員於相同VR情境下之心理壓力反應與應變行為差異
  • 評估VR暴露訓練介入對心理韌性提升與事前心理準備之成效

六、研究方法與步驟

6.1 VR隧道場景建置

延續團隊既有之隧道360度影像資料，透過攝影測量法（SfM-MVS）重建為具真實幾何之三維網格，於Unity中整合火煙粒子特效、動態光影與碰撞系統。

6.2 研究對象

台灣端與日本端各招募一定規模之消防／緊急救護相關人員，依雙方倫理委員會核准之納入排除標準進行招募。

6.3 心理壓力測量工具

  • 生理指標：心率變異度（HRV）、膚電反應（EDA），採即時、非侵入性穿戴式感測器（詳見6.8）
  • 主觀量表：STAI、PSS、自我效能量表
  • 行為科學觀察指標：任務決策反應時間、器材操作正確率、凝視與掃視行為（詳見4.5）

6.4 實驗設計與流程

採基線測量→VR情境暴露→即時生理訊號紀錄→情境後主觀量表填寫之流程；並規劃訓練介入研究，設計前測—VR心理準備訓練介入（依壓力免疫訓練三階段設計，Meichenbaum, 1985）—後測。

6.5 跨國比較設計

台灣與日本兩地採用相同之VR版本與施測流程，並就資料跨境傳輸與隱私處理分別取得雙方倫理委員會核准。

6.6 資料分析方法

考量本研究之目標母體（隧道搜救人員）招募不易，正式樣本規模預期有限，傳統最大概似估計（ML）之結構方程模式在小樣本下容易產生偏誤或不穩定之估計（van de Schoot et al., 2014）。故本計畫規劃採用貝氏結構方程模式（Bayesian SEM）取代傳統SEM來驗證圖2之延伸路徑模型，其優點在於可將既有實證知識轉化為「資訊性先驗」（informative priors）納入模型估計，且在小樣本情境下仍能維持較穩定之參數估計（Muthén & Asparouhov, 2012; van de Schoot et al., 2014）：

  • 先驗來源一（現場實務經驗）：「生理情緒反應→行為表現」路徑之先驗分布，直接依據Dr. Seike團隊已發表之SEM效果量設定（Li et al., 2024b），等於把她既有的實證發現正式轉化為本計畫模型的起始知識，而非從零估計
  • 先驗來源二（專家實務經驗）：「環境壓力源→認知評估」路徑之先驗，參考國內顧問張賢龢教授之隧道應變體系實務經驗，以及計畫主持人本身20年新北市消防局義消暨EMT實務經驗，透過結構化專家訪談轉化為先驗分布之範圍設定
  • 後驗更新與跨國比較：以台灣與日本兩地實際蒐集之資料分別更新後驗分布，並比較兩組後驗參數之可信區間（credible interval）重疊程度，作為H5跨國差異假設之判定依據，取代傳統顯著性檢定之p值判斷
  • 穩健性檢查：同時提供傳統頻率學派SEM與混合設計變異數分析結果作為對照，檢視貝氏與頻率學派結論是否一致

貝氏方法之採用需仰賴計畫主持人與團隊具備相對應之統計軟體操作能力（如R之brms／blavaan套件或Mplus），建議於計畫執行第1年安排團隊成員接受貝氏統計方法之教育訓練，必要時可洽詢具貝氏統計專長之顧問協助模型設定與先驗敏感度分析。

Abstract

Tunnel rescue operations present unique psychological challenges due to confined space, dense smoke, poor visibility, and complex evacuation routes, placing significant stress on rescue personnel — as distinct from general evacuees — that can affect real-time judgment, operational efficiency, and psychological resilience, and may contribute to post-traumatic stress responses. Building on 360° footage of an actual tunnel already captured by our team, this project reconstructs the environment into an interactive VR space through photogrammetry, integrated with fire and smoke simulation, to create a controllable platform for inducing and measuring psychological stress and analyzing stress-related behavioral change from a behavioral-science perspective. In collaboration with Dr. Miho Seike, an internationally recognized researcher on psychological stress in tunnel evacuation, this study will compare physiological stress responses and response behavior between rescue personnel in Taiwan and Japan under the same VR tunnel scenario, and evaluate the effectiveness of VR exposure training in strengthening pre-incident psychological preparedness.

1. Motivation and Significance

Fires and structural collapses in confined environments such as tunnels and underground spaces have long been classified by fire and emergency medical services as high-risk incident types, due to single evacuation routes, rapid smoke spread, and difficulties in communication and localization. Beyond the physical demands they face, first responders in these situations also endure intense psychological stress; from the perspective of the Transactional Model of Stress and Coping (Lazarus & Folkman, 1984), if this stress is not properly regulated through cognitive appraisal and training, it can compromise decision quality, team communication, and long-term mental health.

Tunnel rescue training in Taiwan and Japan still relies mainly on physical drill sites, constrained by site availability, safety risk, and limited repeatability, making it difficult to systematically measure personnel's psychological stress responses under controlled conditions. Advances in VR technology now make controllable, repeatable, low-risk scenario-based training possible; international research has already shown that multisensory VR simulation can effectively enhance the perceived urgency and behavioral realism of evacuation scenarios (Shaw et al., 2019), and also creates an opportunity for cross-national comparative research using identical stimulus conditions.

Our team has already completed 360° photography of an actual tunnel and has the technical foundation to reconstruct it into an interactive VR environment. It is worth emphasizing that the psychological processes triggered by tunnel rescue scenarios differ between "personnel carrying out rescue tasks" and "trapped general road users/the public": existing international literature (e.g., the tunnel evacuation studies of Li et al., 2022, 2024a, 2024b) largely targets "general road users," whose core response is escape and survival; rescue personnel, by contrast, must continue making task judgments, operating equipment, and coordinating as a team while under similar environmental stress, and their psychological resilience and stress tolerance directly affect both rescue effectiveness and their own safety. This project focuses on the psychological stress responses of "rescue personnel" rather than general road users, and examines attention, decision-making, and behavioral change under stress from a behavioral-science perspective (see Theoretical Framework, especially Easterbrook's 1959 cue-utilization theory).

2. Literature Review

(1) International Research: Dr. Miho Seike's Team

Dr. Miho Seike (currently Associate Professor, Hiroshima University; previously affiliated with the University of Tsukuba) is an internationally recognized researcher in tunnel-fire evacuation behavior and psychological-stress measurement. Her team has conducted full-scale smoke-filled tunnel evacuation experiments measuring participants' evacuation walking speed and psychological/emotional state, using structural equation modeling (SEM) to analyze the relationship between negative emotion and walking behavior (Li et al., 2024a — "Negative emotion degree in smoke filled tunnel evacuation," Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology; Li et al., 2024b — "Structural equation modeling of negative emotion and walking behavior," Safety Science), and has recently published work applying machine learning to analyze emotional responses during tunnel evacuation (Seike et al., 2024), as well as a full-scale experiment linking walking speed and psychological state (Li et al., 2026).

Notably, her team has already published a cross-national study comparing evacuation behavior and stress responses of Chinese and Japanese road users in tunnel-fire scenarios (Li et al., 2022), demonstrating direct practical experience and a methodological foundation for cross-national comparative research — closely aligned with the Taiwan–Japan comparison this project intends to conduct. Dr. Seike has also previously participated in a fire-safety assessment of the motorcycle lane in Kaohsiung's Sizihwan (Cross-Harbor) Tunnel in Taiwan, and used video analysis of the 2012 Hsuehshan Tunnel fire as a direct starting point for one of her studies (Chung et al., 2022); she has also published research on heat-release rates in tunnel fires (Seike et al., 2019), showing an existing direct research connection to tunnel issues in Taiwan.

However, her existing research subjects are mostly "general road users/evacuees"; no systematic research has yet addressed the psychological stress responses and pre-incident training needs of "fire/EMS personnel carrying out rescue tasks" in tunnel settings. This is precisely where this project can complement her research and serve as an entry point for extended collaboration.

(2) Domestic (Taiwan) Research

Existing Taiwanese research on firefighters' psychological stress mostly focuses on general duty contexts — for example, a cross-sectional study using the Chinese-version Davidson Trauma Scale (DTS) to examine risk factors for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) among firefighters in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) cases (Chen, 2019), and a questionnaire study on the relationship between firefighters' work stress, leisure coping, and physical/mental health (Wu & Chen, 2020). Methodologically these studies rely mainly on scale-based surveys, with little use of controlled scenario simulation combined with physiological measurement for specific high-risk contexts. Meanwhile, domestic research on road tunnels has concentrated on engineering and management aspects — for example, the development of disaster-response and rescue standard operating procedures for the Hsuehshan Tunnel on the Taipei–Yilan Freeway (Chen, commissioned by the Directorate General of Highways, MOTC; year to be verified) — with subjects again framed as "road users/passengers" rather than the psychological dimension of personnel carrying out rescue tasks. Taken together, Taiwan currently lacks integrated research combining VR scenario simulation, physio-psychological measurement, and training intervention for rescue personnel — a gap this project aims to fill.

(3) Suggested Domestic Advisor: Prof. Hsien-Ho Chang

Prof. Hsien-Ho Chang, Assistant Professor in the Department of Safety and Emergency Services at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University (USA), served in Taiwan's fire service from 2002–2010, during which time he was with the New Taipei City Fire Department and led the Hsuehshan Tunnel fire emergency response plan. 3. Research Gap and Innovation

  • Population innovation: Focuses on "personnel carrying out rescue tasks" rather than general evacuees, complementing rather than duplicating Dr. Seike's existing research (Li et al., 2022, 2024a, 2024b)
  • Methodological innovation: Uses VR instead of real smoke-filled tunnel experiments, allowing controlled scenario variables and substantially reducing participants' actual exposure risk, while drawing on multisensory-simulation literature (Shaw et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2024) to preserve a highly immersive stress-induction effect
  • Applied innovation: Based on Stress Inoculation Training theory (Meichenbaum, 1985), transforms the VR platform into a repeatable pre-incident psychological-preparedness training tool
  • Comparative innovation: Builds on Dr. Seike's team's existing methodological foundation for cross-national comparison (Li et al., 2022), extending the comparison from general road users to professional rescue personnel

4. Theoretical Framework

This project integrates three layers of theory, constructing an overall framework spanning two pathways: "environmental stressor → behavioral performance" and "training intervention."

4.1 Overall Framework: Transactional Model of Stress and Coping (Lazarus & Folkman, 1984)

This theory holds that psychological stress is not caused directly by environmental stimuli, but is jointly mediated by "primary appraisal" (whether this situation constitutes a threat) and "secondary appraisal" (whether one has the resources to cope) — the most representative classic framework in stress research (Lazarus & Folkman, 1984).

4.2 Extending Dr. Seike's Existing Empirical Model

Dr. Seike's team has already used structural equation modeling (SEM) to validate the path "negative emotion → walking speed" (Li et al., 2024b). This project extends that path forward by adding a cognitive-appraisal stage as an antecedent variable, and extends it backward by expanding the behavioral outcome from simple evacuation walking speed to professional task behavior (decision accuracy, equipment-operation performance). The advantage of this extension strategy is that the project does not propose a new model out of thin air, but clearly marks its contribution as an extension of an already internationally published empirical pathway. In addition, the effect sizes already published by Dr. Seike's team will further serve as informative priors for this project's Bayesian statistical analysis (see 6.6), formally translating existing empirical knowledge into part of the model estimation rather than merely citing it in text.

4.3 Training Intervention Framework: Stress Inoculation Training (Meichenbaum, 1985)

This is a classic applied theory in clinical psychology for building psychological resilience, comprising three phases: conceptualization education, skill acquisition and rehearsal, and application and follow-through (Meichenbaum, 1985). This framework maps directly onto the three phases of this project's "VR psychological preparedness training" intervention design.

4.4 Supplementary Variable: Self-Efficacy Theory (Bandura, 1997)

We recommend including self-efficacy in the pre-/post-training psychological scales as a third line of evidence for training effectiveness beyond physiological and behavioral indicators (Bandura, 1997), and it can also serve as a mediating variable between cognitive appraisal and coping behavior in subsequent statistical analysis.

4.5 Extended Measurement Dimension: Attentional Narrowing and Visual Search Behavior (Easterbrook, 1959)

Easterbrook's cue-utilization theory holds that as emotional arousal increases, the range of cues an individual can utilize narrows — a phenomenon often used to explain the decline in information-gathering and decision quality among professionals under high-stress conditions (Easterbrook, 1959). If a VR headset with eye-tracking capability is used (see Section 6.7), gaze-distribution range and saccade-path complexity can be measured as additional behavioral-science evidence supporting the model in Figure 2.

4.6 Research Hypotheses

  • H1: The intensity of environmental stressors is positively correlated with participants' degree of threat appraisal (Lazarus & Folkman, 1984)
  • H2: Degree of threat appraisal is positively correlated with physiological-arousal indicators and subjective negative emotion
  • H3: The degree of physiological and emotional response is negatively correlated with task behavioral performance; this pathway extends Dr. Seike's team's existing SEM empirical model (Li et al., 2024b)
  • H4: Participants who receive VR psychological preparedness training show significantly lower post-training psychological stress responses than pre-training, and significantly increased self-efficacy (Meichenbaum, 1985; Bandura, 1997)
  • H5: Taiwanese and Japanese participants show differing patterns of psychological stress response and task behavioral performance under the same VR scenario, as determined by the degree of overlap in Bayesian posterior credible intervals (echoing the cross-national comparison findings of Li et al., 2022)

5. Research Objectives

  • Build an interactive, scenario-variable-adjustable VR platform for inducing and testing tunnel-related psychological stress
  • Establish a standardized protocol for physiological and subjective measurement of psychological stress in rescue personnel
  • Analyze rescue personnel's attention, decision-making, and behavioral change under high-stress conditions from a behavioral-science perspective
  • Compare psychological stress responses and response behavior between Taiwanese and Japanese rescue personnel under the same VR scenario
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of VR exposure training in enhancing psychological resilience and pre-incident preparedness

6. Methods and Procedures

6.1 VR Tunnel Scene Construction

Building on the team's existing 360° tunnel imagery, we will use photogrammetry (SfM-MVS) to reconstruct it into a geometrically accurate 3D mesh, integrating fire/smoke particle effects, dynamic lighting, and collision systems in Unity.

6.2 Study Participants

Fire/EMS personnel will be recruited in Taiwan and Japan respectively, following inclusion/exclusion criteria approved by each side's ethics committee.

6.3 Psychological Stress Measurement Tools

  • Physiological indicators: heart rate variability (HRV), electrodermal activity (EDA), using real-time, non-invasive wearable sensors (see 6.8)
  • Subjective scales: STAI, PSS, self-efficacy scale
  • Behavioral-science observational indicators: task decision reaction time, equipment-operation accuracy, gaze and saccade behavior (see 4.5)

6.4 Experimental Design and Procedure

The protocol follows: baseline measurement → VR scenario exposure → real-time physiological signal recording → post-scenario subjective scale completion; a training-intervention study is also planned, with a pre-test — VR psychological preparedness training intervention (designed per the three phases of Stress Inoculation Training, Meichenbaum, 1985) — post-test design.

6.5 Cross-National Comparative Design

Taiwan and Japan will use the same VR version and testing protocol, with ethics approval obtained separately from each side's ethics committee for cross-border data transfer and privacy handling.

6.6 Data Analysis Method

Given that the target population for this study (tunnel rescue personnel) is difficult to recruit and the formal sample size is expected to be limited, conventional maximum-likelihood (ML) structural equation modeling is prone to biased or unstable estimates with small samples (van de Schoot et al., 2014). This project therefore plans to use Bayesian Structural Equation Modeling (Bayesian SEM) instead of conventional SEM to validate the extended path model in Figure 2. Its advantage is that existing empirical knowledge can be converted into "informative priors" incorporated into model estimation, maintaining more stable parameter estimates even in small-sample settings (Muthén & Asparouhov, 2012; van de Schoot et al., 2014):

  • Prior source 1 (field empirical evidence): The prior distribution for the "physiological/emotional response → behavioral performance" path is set directly from the SEM effect sizes already published by Dr. Seike's team (Li et al., 2024b) — formally converting her existing empirical findings into the starting knowledge for this project's model, rather than estimating from scratch
  • Prior source 2 (expert field experience): The prior for the "environmental stressor → cognitive appraisal" path draws on the practical tunnel-response-system experience of domestic advisor Prof. Hsien-Ho Chang, as well as the PI's own 20 years of experience as a volunteer EMT with the New Taipei City Fire Department, translated into prior-distribution ranges through structured expert interviews
  • Posterior updating and cross-national comparison: Posterior distributions will be updated separately using data actually collected in Taiwan and Japan, and the degree of overlap between the two groups' posterior credible intervals will be compared as the basis for testing hypothesis H5, replacing conventional p-value-based significance testing
  • Robustness check: Conventional frequentist SEM and mixed-design ANOVA results will also be provided for comparison, to check whether Bayesian and frequentist conclusions are consistent

Adopting Bayesian methods requires the PI and team to have corresponding statistical software skills (e.g., R's brms/blavaan packages or Mplus); we recommend arranging training in Bayesian statistical methods for team members in Year 1 of the project, and consulting an advisor with Bayesian-statistics expertise for model specification and prior-sensitivity analysis if needed.

6.9 Temperature and Humidity Simulation Equipment

Drawing on international multisensory VR training literature, low-risk devices will be used to simulate heat and humidity:

  • Infrared radiant heat lamp: simulates radiant heat from fire, an approach already seen in multisensory VR evacuation research in the academic literature (Shaw et al., 2019)
  • Ultrasonic humidifier/mister: simulates humidity and fine moisture sensation, drawing on the misting-device design used in robotic-arm multisensory simulation research (Chen et al., 2024)
  • Control method: Arduino/ESP32 controlling via relay modules, synchronized with Unity scenario trigger points

6.10 VR Omnidirectional Walking Device

We recommend acquiring a commercially available omnidirectional VR treadmill such as the KAT Walk C2 Core (~US$799, ~NT$25,000), which uses optical foot tracking and is compatible with PC VR and, via bridging software, the Meta Quest system.

6.11 Fire Nozzle and VR Controller Integration Plan

  • Position tracking: A VR controller will be embedded inside the nozzle grip — the same principle used in commercially available VR fire-training simulators in Japan
  • Rotation-angle sensing: A rotary encoder will be fitted to the nozzle's spray-pattern adjustment ring, directly following the approach in the academic literature (Jeon et al., 2021), which mounted a rotary encoder on the nozzle-adjustment section of a fire hose
  • Commercial reference case: An HTC Vive Tracker attached to the end of a fire hose to track its movement, as seen in the commercial system FLAIM Trainer
